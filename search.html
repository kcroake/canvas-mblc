Accessibility

Equitable access around and within a building for everyone through elimination of barriers in compliance with the Americans with Disability Act (ADA).

Addition Project

Work required to increase the overall external dimensions of a public library facility but that will not touch the existing building, which presumably has been well-maintained and received necessary upgrades. Considerations while contemplating this project type include suitability for service areas, logical adjacencies of service areas, structural capacity for collections and assembly, viability of and cohesion of connection point, accessibility, sustainability, and viability of an alternative location during construction.

Addition/Renovation Project

Work that involves the entire existing facility and which will result in an increase in the overall external dimensions of a public library facility. If the existing facility has historic value and/or significance to the community and the site allows for expansion of space to serve the community, this project type may be suitable. It allows for lower embodied carbon impacts with use or reuse of existing materials. Considerations while contemplating this project type include suitability for service areas, logical adjacencies of service areas, structural capacity for collections and assembly, viability of and cohesion of connection point, accessibility, sustainability, and viability of an alternative location during construction.

Adjacency

A condition in which two spaces directly connect to each other.

Area Descriptions

An element of the library building program that details each room or area within the future building that fits under Net Area. Includes types and number of seats; collection types and number of volumes; shelving preferences; other furniture; equipment; storage needs; requirements for lighting, acoustics, technology, signage, and user experience; desired adjacencies to, proximities to, and distances from other areas; needed occupancy; and estimated square footage to accommodate all listed items within the space.

Circulation

An architectural term referring to the spaces that allow people to move around. Corridors and stairs are considered “circulation spaces”.

Condition Assessment

A written report completed by a building sciences firm, a registered engineer, or a registered architect regarding the physical condition of an existing facility’s building, site, and grounds as well as future financial risk or liability issues. Elements are evaluated for condition, completion, code compliance, life-cycle costs, appropriateness for intended use, and any issues compromising the structure or Systems.

Embodied Carbon

The amount of CO2 greenhouse gas emissions released in the lifetime of a product, including manufacturing, transportation, useful life, and disposal/recycling of that product.

Grade Change

The difference in elevation across an area of land, for example from the top to the bottom of a hill.

Gross Area

The total area of a building or space, including the Net Area (see Net Area), the wall thicknesses, the janitorial and mechanical spaces that may be part of the space and/or building. Gross Area is expressed in Gross Square Feet (GSF).

Needs Assessment

A bridging document between the Strategic Plan (and the 30-year demographic projections) and the Building Program, it analyzes and defines the needs of the community in terms of library service and translates them into concrete, physical terms for the facility. Analysis is not tied to the current facility (or any other particular site) but instead identifies where the gaps are. As a statement of needs, it does not speculate about possible solutions.

Net Area (also Assignable Area or Programmatic Area)

The total area inside the library’s walls and used for library-specific functions, excluding janitorial and mechanical areas.

New Construction

Work which will result in a new building on the existing or a new site. While this project type allows for optimization of spaces to meet codes and needs, it also involves the most embodied carbon of the three whole building project types. Considerations while contemplating this project type include the site’s convenience for users, suitability for accommodating the building and parking, and access to public transit, as well as repurposing opportunities of the former library building if it is not being razed.

Library Building Consultant

A professional librarian with expertise in community engagement, needs assessments, space planning, and library building projects who assists with information gathering for and/or drafting the library building program.

Life-Cycle Cost Analysis

A study of initial costs of a material or system compared to costs of performance, maintenance, and replacement over the lifespan of the item.

Owner’s Project Manager

A professional who meets state qualifications to represent the building owner from predesign through post construction phases by providing independent and competent advice on all aspects of a building project and who is hired through adherence with designer selection law. Responsibilities include assistance with
designer selection, participation in cost estimating, general contractor and subcontractor prequalification, negotiations with the designer and the general contractor, monitoring of all phases of design and construction, regular communication with the library director, and assistance with any monthly and final grant reporting. Public awarding authorities in MA are required to engage the services of an owner’s project manager (OPM) for all phases of any building project estimated to cost $1.5 million or more. The OPM must be hired before the project designer.

Partial Building Project

A partial building project can involve construction but does not touch the entire building. The project types under partial building projects include refurnishing, single purpose, and addition.

Program

Other than in relation to the Library Building Program, the term denotes an activity, event, etc. for the public hosted or implemented by the library.

Project Type

The scale and scope of a project under one of two umbrella categories: Partial Building Project and Whole Building Project.

Prototyping

Experimenting with different configurations, adjacencies, and/or elements to understand user preferences prior to making a permanent change or purchase while utilizing existing or acquired elements that are full-size or model scale.

Proximity

A condition in which two spaces are near each other, but direct connection is not absolutely required (compared to Adjacency).

Refurnishing

Work required if the current configuration accommodates the service areas needed but a refresh in furniture, fixtures, and equipment is necessary. For a cohesive design that balances factors such as budget, environmentally-friendly pieces, and durability, consider hiring an interior designer.

Renovation Project

Work required to restore and modernize a public library building in order that the facility may be effectively utilized for its designated functional purpose and comply with current code requirements. Such projects consist of work of such scope that, if not completed, the present facility would require complete building replacement. This project type is for the library that does not need additional space to serve the community. A renovation can lower embodied carbon impacts by using/reusing existing materials. Considerations while weighing this project type include suitability for needed service areas, logical adjacencies of service areas, structural capacity for collections and assembly, flexibility, accessibility, sustainability, and viability of an alternative location during construction.

Resilience

Design decisions that bolster the durability of a building and/or site.

Sightlines

An open visual corridor between two or more points.

Single Purpose Project:

A project that focuses on one element or aspect of the building or grounds. Examples include:

  • Interior Spaces with Minimal Construction: Work required if the current configuration does not accommodate the service areas needed. Moving walls and room adjacencies within the existing footprint requires architectural services.
  • Building Envelope and Systems Upgrades: Work required to protect the building from the elements and/or to meet code requirements. With each building envelope repair or upgrade, consider what work is necessary to improve efficiency and resilience. Engineering firms and/or architectural firms who do small projects are typically retained for such projects. Depending on the scope of work, an alternative location for operations may be advisable.
  • Site: Work required on the site to meet code requirements or a project to provide services outside. Accessibility and protection from the elements should be considered. Engineering and/or landscape architecture services may need to be retained.

Sustainability

Design decisions that are environmentally-conscious, energy-efficient, socially responsible, and cost-effective.

Universal Design

Design that goes beyond compliance with code and results in an environment that is accessible by the widest range of users, benefitting many. An example would be an automatic door, which not only creates an accessible entry for individuals using assistive-mobility devices but also for individuals with strollers, a dolly, bags and/or packages, etc.

Value Engineering

The practice of cutting costs that is typically performed after receiving an estimate to keep the project within budget. It can include eliminating a scope of work, choosing more economical options for furniture, fixtures, and/or equipment without sacrificing durability, etc.

Whole Building Project

A whole building project touches every wall, ceiling, and surface. The project types under whole building projects include renovation, addition/renovation, and new construction.
